## Meeting the Children and Families We Support

In September, an international group of supporters of Tumaini Education Initiative (TEI) visited Bukoba and the surrounding area to meet the people involved in TEI, to see the schools attended by the children we support, and learn more about their families and circumstances. The group consisted of Father Nicolaus who organised the visit, Father Hans Hammerl and parishioners from Bad Goisern, Austria, and Don Claudio Monti from Lanzo d'Intelvi, Italy and the three TEI supporters from Markt Schwaben, Germany.

From the moment we arrived, we were welcomed with extraordinary warmth. At the airport in Bukoba, members of the TEI community and the local Church, including the Bishop were there to greet us. At Bunazi, the local choir welcomed us with singing, dancing and palm branches. This continued throughout our visit: pupils sang for us at their schools, and parish communities made us feel part of their celebrations. We were deeply touched by the hospitality we received.

We met all of the children supported by TEI and visited some at home. Some families are living in very difficult circumstances, without running water and with limited means to provide even the essentials. We met children living with elderly grandparents, as well as children whose families have been disrupted by bereavement or separation. These visits were a powerful reminder that encouragement, regular contact and an understanding of their home circumstances are all required to ensure these children's educational success.

## What We Saw in the Schools

We visited both a privately run and two government primary school. The differences in resources were striking. At one government school, nearly 600 pupils were being taught by just seven teachers, with two volunteer teachers providing additional help. Classrooms and uniforms showed the effects of limited resources.

One headteacher told us that children supported by TEI were doing particularly well in their classes. However, she was concerned that some pupils might struggle when they moved on to secondary school, especially if they had not developed sufficient English-language skills. Primary education in Tanzania is in Swahili, while secondary education is in English. The visit reinforced how important it is not only to help children attend school, but also to give them the support they need to make lasting progress.

At one government school, we saw a classroom with cracks in the walls and signs that the building was sinking. A geologist in our group identified the cause: rainwater from the roof was draining towards the building and soaking the ground beneath it. He believed that very simple measures, moving some earth to redirect the drainage flow would address the problem.

We already cooperate with the schools attended by our supported children, for example to identify who is most in need, but this also
raised an idea worth exploring: schools could identify practical projects—such as repairing a classroom, and TEI could consider supporting selected projects in partnership with the school. Such work would need careful planning and agreement with local partners, but relatively small improvements could make a tangible difference to pupils’ daily learning environment.

At the privately run school we also learnt about construction costs, saw the dormitories for the boarders and the dining hall. Most of the material costs had been donated by donors in USA. For example a USD 20,000 donation for the dining/meeting hall.

## Clothing, School Supplies and Local Volunteers

In July before the trip, Eine-Welt-Gruppe headed by Christoph Altmann at the Franz Marc Gymnasium in Germany organised a collection of sports goods and clothes and school supplies for TEI. It was about 140kg which we carried in our suitcases. At Bunazi parish, children were able to choose clothes that fitted them. They were delighted. Remaining clothing was distributed after the visit.

We also visited the TEI office and reviewed examples of the project’s records, including school results, receipts and financial reports. The documentation is very well organised thanks to Father Salvius and the local volunteers.

Their work is essential. One volunteer told us about balancing her job and family responsibilities with visits to the children and families supported by TEI. These visits help the project stay informed about how children are doing and what difficulties they may be facing. We came away with great respect for the commitment of the people who keep this work going day by day.

## Growing the number of sponsored children

As a concrete result of the visit many of our fellow travellers and other people from Lanzo and Bad Goisern decided to support TEI by sponsoring children. This way we expect that all of our currently supported children and also all those that were on the waiting list should have sponsors starting with the new school year in January.


## A Long-Term Vision: A Church-Owned Secondary School

An important part of our discussions concerned the proposal to establish a private, Church-owned secondary school. The aim is to create a sustainable source of income that can help fund scholarships for disadvantaged students over the long term.

We visited the proposed site, approximately 37 acres of land behind a church near the main road towards Uganda. The location offers considerable space for a school and, in time, potentially for staff housing and other facilities. The Church has used the land for around 50 years. Formal confirmation of ownership, planning permission, approval from the local council and detailed building plans will all be required before construction can begin.

The initial phase discussed would include classrooms, three laboratories, an office for the school director and boarding accommodation. There is still a great deal to prepare, and construction is not yet ready to start. Nevertheless, seeing the land helped us understand the scale of the opportunity and the work ahead. We have already started receiving donations and pledges of support for the school and are setting up an account that is separate from the sponsorships.

We visited a primary boarding school with around 250 pupils and 15 teachers. Its classrooms and facilities showed what can be achieved when a school has a stronger resource base. The visit gave us useful points of comparison as we consider what a future school might need.

In Bunazi, there are very many elementary schools but just one state run secondary, that is extremely resource constrained. A private alternative that offers boarding and scholarships for pupils from disadvantaged families is definitely needed. The initiative is strongly supported by the local church who donated the land, and is also supported by the local council.

## Building Support That Lasts

The  sponsorship programme is being developed so that individuals may be able to support a particular child and, where appropriate, receive updates on their progress. These individual long term commitments ensure we have a long lived initiative that will support these children throughout their education. Our vision is to educate leaders that continue this work, and make lasting change in their communities.

Alongside individual sponsorship, our main effort will be the new school project. Without it any supported child needs to find places in distant boarding schools once they moved to the secondary education. In addition the scholarships will help turn TEI into a self sustaining initiative.

We will also explore the possibility of cooperating more closely with the public elementary schools attended by TEI supported children.

Above all, the visit brought the purpose of TEI into sharp focus. Behind every school report and school-fee payment is a child with a family, a story and the potential to build a different future. We are grateful to everyone in Tanzania and abroad who contributes to this work.
